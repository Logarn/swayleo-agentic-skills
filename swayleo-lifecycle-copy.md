# Lifecycle Copy

## Purpose and provenance

Expanded from the starter; informed by recovered brand strategy and copy-review practices.

This workflow applies across brands by substituting the current brand's sources, account, scope and approval rules. It is a reusable instruction set, not evidence that a live system is configured.

## Inputs

Brand voice and product evidence; audience/lifecycle stage; approved job and angle; offer terms; destination; required format; existing copy and comments.

If an input is unavailable, identify its effect and continue work that does not depend on it. Do not fill factual gaps with another brand's assumptions.

## Workflow

1. Read the brand context and current brief before drafting. Identify the reader's situation, the single job of the message and the action it should earn.
2. Separate confirmed product facts, approved claims and creative angles. Never invent clinical results, testimonials, discounts, scarcity, founder identity or delivery guarantees.
3. Structure the message around Lead, Support, Proof and Close. Support explains the benefit; proof is attributed and scoped to the actual product or study. The close makes the next action clear.
4. Write complementary subject and preview text. Avoid repeating the same wording in both. Provide variants only when requested or useful for a defined test.
5. Match the brand's voice and reader awareness. Use clear language, concrete detail and short sections. Do not import another brand's voice, benefit claims or purchase terms.
6. Keep recurring purchase terms, offer exclusions, guarantee limitations and required disclaimers visible in the appropriate place. Flag locale-specific compliance review rather than claiming legal approval.
7. Preserve merge tags and event variables. Specify fallbacks for missing names/products and distinguish static recommendations from dynamic cart content.
8. In an existing document, use the requested table structure and current client-specific override. Strategy-only work does not authorize filling blank Copy/Inspo cells.
9. Finish with a claims/offer/destination check. Present unresolved facts before calling the draft ready for review. Drafting never implies permission to send.

## Deliverable

Email: campaign ID, subject, preview, lead/header, body sections, CTA label/destination and relevant disclaimer. SMS: message, destination and flagged consent/opt-out requirements. Include open facts separately.

Use the output worksheet inside the downloadable library ZIP to structure a working draft. Adapt it to the requested deliverable; do not create extra files unless useful.

## Example

For checkout recovery, return the shopper to their actual checkout. A subscription-benefit paragraph is not appropriate for every one-time buyer.

## Verification and completion

The message has one job; claims and terms match evidence; proof fits the product; CTA matches destination; variables have fallbacks; review state is explicit.

Report what was completed, the source or saved record that proves it, and any unresolved blocker. Distinguish a draft, a saved change and a live action. Publishing, posting, scheduling or changing live systems requires the corresponding user request and available tools.
